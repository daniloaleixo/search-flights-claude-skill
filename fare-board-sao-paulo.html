What the board does not know

Every limit that could change a decision, stated rather than smoothed over.

  • Origins backfilled

    BER (8 sweep fares, allows 2 stops against the sweep's 1), HAM (3 sweep fares) were searched alone at their own stop budget. A search is backfilled either because the sweep returned nothing for the origin, or because the origin's stop budget is wider than the single limit a sweep search can carry, so its extra stops were never tried until backfill. Those fares came from a different search than the rest of the board.

  • Coverage

    Every origin returned a usable result page.

  • Rows never expanded

    357 of 462 rows were never opened, so their layovers and their night status are unknown. They show as "not checked", which is not the same as clean: AMS 974, FRA 975, AMS 981, FRA 986, FRA 986, FRA 989, FRA 989, FRA 992, FRA 992, FRA 992, FRA 992, FRA 992 and 345 more.

  • Stop limit each search ran under

    AMS at most 1 stop, BER at most 2 stops, FRA at most 1 stop, HAM at most 1 stop, MUC at most 1 stop, PRG at most 1 stop. A sweep carries one stop limit, so it runs at the tightest budget across all origins; an origin allowed more stops only sees them in its own backfill search.

  • No open jaws in this run

    Every fare here is a round trip that comes back to the airport it left from. Open jaws were dropped because Google prices them as multi-city searches, and a multi-city result page carries no Cheapest tab at all, only the Best set. On the one pair measured that gap was 1159 EUR against 879 EUR for the same search. The airport by airport grid therefore only has a diagonal.

  • Which clock the times are on

    The out and back columns carry the departure and arrival times, and each one is local to its own airport: a flight that leaves at 10:05 and lands at 06:10 marked "+1" lands the next morning, and the gap between the two clocks is not the length of the flight. The duration column is that. The outbound times come with every row Google returned. The return times only exist for the 2 of 462 rows whose return end was actually opened; the rest say "times not opened", which is why nobody knows them, not a claim that the flight has none.

  • The ground journey is not in the fare

    Every fare on this board is a number Google returned and nothing has been added to it. Beside it sits the door-to-door figure: that fare plus the train at both ends, and a hotel where the flight cannot be reached on the day. Comparisons here read that figure, not the fare, because an airport six hours away sells a fare that is not the price of going there. 462 of 462 rows have one. The train fares are the ones the traveller quoted and are treated as prices, not estimates, so the door-to-door figure is a single number rather than a range.

  • What the train and the hotel are assumed to cost

    AMS 60 EUR and 7h, hotel 120, BER 0 EUR and 1h, FRA 37 EUR and 4.5h, hotel 90, HAM 37 EUR and 2.5h, hotel 80, MUC 37 EUR and 5h, hotel 95, PRG 29 EUR and 5h, hotel 60. Hours are front door to terminal, so they include getting across town at both ends. A flight counts as out of reach on the day when leaving home in time for it would mean a train before the first one; coming back, when landing leaves less than 1.5h before the last train home. The hotel figures are estimates, which is why the switch at the top exists: turn it off and every figure on the board is recomputed without them. The hours never change with it. A night spent near the airport is spent whoever paid for the bed.

  • A night in transit is a night too

    A layover that runs through the night and lasts at least 8 hours is priced as a bed, not as time in a terminal, and that cost sits in the door-to-door figure with everything else. 37 rows carry one. Assumed prices: AMS 120, BCN 85, CMN 45, FCO 80, FOR 40, LIS 70. Anywhere else is assumed to cost 90. Shorter night layovers carry nothing: leaving the airport and coming back is not worth it, so they stay a hard night in a seat, which the night verdict already judges. The switch at the top turns these off with the others.

  • Flights you cannot reach on the day

    174 rows depart too early to get to by train that morning, so they carry the evening before and a night near the airport: AMS 11:55, AMS 11:55, AMS 11:55, AMS 11:55, FRA 06:00, FRA 06:00, FRA 06:00, FRA 06:00…. Those hours are inside the door-to-door duration whether or not the bed is paid for.

  • Return ends nobody opened

    A search result row describes the outbound flight only: the return times live behind a second click. 460 of 462 rows still have no return arrival, so nothing has been judged about getting home from them, and they carry no hotel at that end. That is an unknown, not an all-clear.

  • Rows that had vanished by the time we went back

    2 rows were cheap enough to need expanding, but the itinerary was no longer in the results when we returned for it. Fares and result sets move between the capture pass and the expansion pass. These rows keep the fare they were captured at and will never get a night verdict: FRA 975, BER 1020.

  • Self-transfer fares

    10 of 462 rows are self transfers: two separate tickets, so a missed connection is yours to pay for and bags do not through-check. BER 968, BER 1044, AMS 1344, AMS 1406, AMS 1423, AMS 1527, FRA 1608, BER 1641.

  • What a row's link actually opens

    Each row's link is the search it came from, not a bookmark to that one fare: a sweep search carries every origin and one date pair, so opening it reopens the whole result list, and the fare has to be found again inside it, sorted by price. It is not a permalink to a specific itinerary.
